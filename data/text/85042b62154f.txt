Mi participación en los ENDES 2022 — 8 — Soy Ana Paola Ortiz Reyes, tengo 14 años, y formo parte del Grupo XI de la Provincia Puebla. Realmente no llevo mucho tiempo en los Scouts, inicié como Scout hace ya algunos meses, aproximadamente en junio de este mismo año. Me considero una persona que logra lo que se propone y que cada día se esfuerza para ser mejor, así es como terminé aquí, buscando nuevas experiencias y teniendo grandes responsabilidades como ser Subguía en mi Patrulla. Hay muchos términos de los que no tengo muy claro el significado, un claro ejemplo es la palabra “Scout”, ¿realmente qué significa ser un buen Scout? Bueno cada persona puede tener su propia definición, pero personalmente creo que ser Scout te ayuda a sacar lo mejor de ti mismo, implica responsabilidades, así como también diversión, significa vivir nuevas experiencias y aprender cosas nuevas, ser Scout es tener un lugar seguro para poder convivir con muchas personas a las que le importas, formar parte de una gran familia que te apoya en cualquier momento, en las buenas y en las malas, para mí eso es ser Scout y estoy muy orgullosa de poder decir que soy una. Ir a competir en disciplinas que me gustan y he aspirado, poner en alto de dónde vengo y de dónde soy, y orgullecer a las personas que me han apoyado; ese era uno de mis mayores sueños y gracias a este evento al fin lo pude cumplir. El “Encuentro Nacional Deportivo Scout” (ENDES) fue mi segundo campamento e indudablemente el mejor de ellos. Tengo que admitir que me encantan los deportes, por esa razón acepté participar de este evento a último momento con una permuta que me dio Cristian Báez, un gran compañero y un buen amigo, ese tipo de amigo que te ayuda en todo momento y que jamás te abandona. Prakash Raj Pandey’s Journey to the Top of the World His dream is about to come true. Since he was a little boy, Prakash Raj Pandey had been longing to do what thousands of people have done before: climb to the top of the largest mountain in the world, Mount Everest. Climbing Mt. Everest is one of the most dangerous feats that a person can ever take. Hundreds of people have died climbing its treacherous, rigid, and harsh conditions, but Prakash would not let anything keep him from reaching his goal. Prakash was the team leader of an 11-member expedition, organized by Pioneer Adventure Pvt. Ltd. Throughout his journey, Prakash made sure to carry the “World Organization of Scouting Movement” (WOSM), and “Messengers of Peace” (MOP) flags, along with the flag of his native country, Nepal. He kept the flags on a pole so that other scouts from different countries could easily recognize him as a scout. During his trek, he met scouts from different countries who had come to climb Mt. Everest or to do research. The team set out on April 13, 2018, traveling from Kathmandu to Lukla. Six days later, they reached Everest Base Camp, at a height of 5,364 meters above sea level. After resting, their guide trained them on essential information, such as how to ice climb, how to wear and use safety equipment, how to use oxygen, and what problems may occur and what symptoms to look for while climbing at high altitude. During the following month, Prakash faced several obstacles on his journey to the top of Mt. Everest. With the help of his guide, Chiring Bhote Lama, Prakash barely missed an avalanche. The team camped at several different sites. They crossed rugged terrain and delt with extreme weather conditions. They had times when they needed to push forward without rest. In all of these situations, Prakash never thought about turning back. — 22 — "If there are no obstacles and challenges in life, There is no success and opportunities in life" Prakash Raj Pandey, mountaineer En tu revista Foro Joven ¡nos ponemos internacionales! ¡Esperemos que disfrutes nuestro primer artículo en inglés y no te olvides de mandar tus comentarios a través de nuestras redes sociales!

He felt that he had a great responsibility, because he was “carrying the WOSM and MOP flags for the first time to the top of the world…I needed to make history, which would make every scout proud.” On May 15th, Prakash and the team had reached South Cole, at a height of 8,000 meters. From his own words, “At 9:30 in the evening, we started our final summit push with the Nepal national flag, WOSM flag and MOP flag. My guide, Chiring Bhote Lama and I, fighting for our dream at the point where we were. With passing all the traffic and weather challenge at May 16th at 11:30 am, I reached the summit. Then, I waved the WOSM flag and MOP flag representing scouts from all over the world. At that time I was beyond the limit of my happiness. Of course, I was tired but I was feeling a different kind of energy from my inner heart, I could say that was the spirit of scouting. Even though I had tears in my eyes, I was so happy. Finally, my dream came true; A dream that was not only mine, but of scouts from all around the world.” Prakash feels that “It does not matter about life but you have to be at the top. This is what every climber wants to achieve in the mountain, and of course, I got that courage and spirit from my scouting life.” So far, Prakash has climbed Baden Powell Scout Peak twice (5,890m), Island Peak (6,160m), Mt. Manaslu (8,163m), Mt. Amadablam known as the “Killer Mountain” (6,812m), and now Mt. Everest (8848.9m). Now, he wants to climb all of the highest mountains of each of the seven continents, known as the “Seven Summits,” including Mt. Everest again. “I would like to express my sincere gratitude especially to my family, great supporters who directly and indirectly engaged on my mission and my well-wisher, and of course the World Scout Asia-Pacific Region and Messengers of Peace for huge support, encouraged and trusted in me,” Prakash said. “This would not had been a success, if I have not had the huge support. Thanks a lot everyone.” One last thought, “I believe the flags I waved on the top of the world spread the message of peace all over the world. Together we can create a better world.” — 23 — "Mountains are God and Sherpas are the worshipper of the mountains" - Prakash Raj Pandey
